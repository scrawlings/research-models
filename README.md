# research-models

[Environmental changes, co-extinction, and patterns in the fossil record](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/9804102) Luis A. N. Amaral (MIT), Martin Meyer (BU) 1998
The finding here takes a trophic network organised in layers. Each layer feeds only on part of the layer below it. Adds a model for speciation. And extintion event that cascade, (if enough of your food fails then you also go extinct). It shows the size of these cascading failures follows a power law in a way that has very suggestive parallels with the scale of extinction events in the paleological record. [trophic-cascade-extinctions.html](trophic-cascade-extinctions.html)

[Inequity Aversion and the Evolution of Cooperation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.4946) Asrar Ahmed, Kamalakar Karlapalem 2014
The findings here take the idea that a defector in the prisoner's dilemma is likely to accumulate resource. So by tagging each agent in repeated play with it's "wealth", and having an inequity aversion property which is managed by a genetic algorithm, and giving agents the choice not to play which is based in their degree of inequality aversion being compared to the defector's resource hoard, we get a system where agents can evolve to cooperate by recognising and avoiding non-cooperators based on a simple visible factor and without needing to know and remember their history. And even in pools with low numbers of weak cooperators to begin with they soon proliferate and become more coorperative. [inequity-aversion-cooperation.html](inequity-aversion-cooperation.html)
This is cool for all sorts of reasons, it's a model of behaviour that is remarkably low cost if we consider how it might correlated to real world factors. And it robustly tends toward toward cooperation. Inequity aversion and cooperative behaviour are frequently observed in nature, and having a low cost mechanism in a model that produce them is telling.
