Inequity Aversion & the Evolution of Cooperation

An interactive model after Ahmed & Karlapalem (2014), "Inequity Aversion and the Evolution of Cooperation" (arXiv:1402.4946). Agents never see a partner's type or history — only their own accumulated payoff. That single number acts as a proxy signal: agents avoid partners whose accumulated payoff differs too much from their own, which lets cooperators cluster together and out-compete defectors, purely through partner selection.

The model

Each agent is a fixed type (always-cooperate or always-defect) and carries a sensitivity parameter λ ∈ [0, λmax] describing how averse it is to payoff inequality. At the start of every generation everyone's accumulated payoff r resets to 0. Agents are then visited in random order; each samples a set of candidate partners (a random subset in the well-mixed model, or its 4 grid neighbours in the lattice model) and prefers whichever candidate has the closest accumulated payoff to its own — weighted by how inequity-averse it is:

φ(i, j) = exp( −λᵢ · |rᵢ − rⱼ| )probability agent i would accept j as a partner
P(i, j) = φ(i, j) · φ(j, i) = exp( −(λᵢ + λⱼ) · |rᵢ − rⱼ| )probability the interaction actually happens (mutual consent)

If the interaction happens, the pair plays one round of the Prisoner's Dilemma with the normalized payoff C-C → (1, 1), C-D → (0, 1+c/b), D-D → (c/b, c/b) — a defector always earns more than a cooperator in any single encounter. At the end of the generation, agents reproduce via binary tournament: two random agents (globally in the well-mixed model, from the local neighbourhood in the lattice model) are compared by accumulated payoff, and the winner's type and λ are copied into the next generation, subject to mutation.

Why cooperation emerges without memory or reputation

Nobody ever recognises anybody. Early in a generation every payoff is 0, so everyone looks alike and cooperators are briefly vulnerable to exploitation. But the moment a defector free-rides on a cooperator, their payoffs diverge. If the cooperator is sufficiently inequity-averse (λ large enough), it stops accepting that defector — and any other agent whose payoff has drifted away from its own — and starts clustering with agents whose payoff still matches its own, which in practice means other cooperators. Those clusters earn the mutual-cooperation payoff of 1 each round instead of being drained by defectors, out-reproduce everyone else, and λ itself rises under selection. Eventually tolerant cooperators (small λ) become slightly favoured again since defectors are now rare, λ drifts back down, and the population becomes vulnerable to a fresh wave of defection — producing the cyclical dynamics visible in the fc chart above. Try lowering the search space Ns or raising c/b to see cooperation struggle to recover; switch to the 2D lattice to see spatial clustering make cooperation far more stable.
